Scientific literacy

Technical content built around methods, identity and evidence.

The public library explains analytical concepts and research documentation without teaching personal dosing, reconstitution, administration or treatment use.

CAS, molecular formula and molecular weight

How core chemical identifiers help distinguish materials and reduce naming ambiguity across catalogs, certificates and laboratory records.

Chromatography

What HPLC can tell you

Why chromatographic purity should be tied to a method, sample, report and lot rather than reduced to an unsupported marketing percentage.

Mass analysis

What LC-MS can tell you

How mass-based identity evidence differs from chromatographic purity and why neither result should be stretched beyond what the method supports.

Documentation

How to read a COA

Look for sample identity, lot number, test method, laboratory, date, result, units and any limitations stated on the report.

Traceability

Why lot numbers matter

Lot identity should connect the physical material to its analytical documents and order history. A certificate from another batch is not interchangeable.

Safety documents

What an SDS is for

Safety Data Sheets communicate handling, hazard, storage and response information. They are not proof of clinical safety or efficacy.

Source diligence

Questions to ask a supplier

Who produced the material? Which lot is being offered? What documentation exists? Which laboratory performed testing? Are source records consistent?

Research boundaries

Technical information vs. personal-use instructions

Chemistry, methods and model-system literature belong in the research library. Dosing, injection, treatment and self-use protocols do not.

Publication standard

When a product record is ready

Identity, amount, price, lot status, source documentation and analytical status should be known before a material is represented as an active catalog SKU.

Member layer

Technical catalog records sit behind research access.

Public education stays non-clinical. Members who certify a lawful research purpose can access product-specific records and research-order controls.
