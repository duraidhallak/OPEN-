Safety standards

A reliable source is only one piece of the puzzle.

Quality, legal status, prescribing, dispensing, labeling, advertising, and patient suitability are separate questions. All of them matter.

1. Product status

Confirm whether the exact medication is FDA-approved, lawfully compounded for a patient-specific need, or otherwise eligible for the intended human-use pathway.

2. Source and quality

Verify the actual manufacturer or pharmacy, lot records, testing, storage, handling, and chain of custody. Never invent laboratory results or imply testing that did not occur.

3. Prescribing

Prescription medications require an appropriate clinician relationship and a valid prescription. The website does not replace medical judgment.

4. Dispensing

Medication must be dispensed through an appropriately licensed pharmacy or other lawful channel. A marketing site should not imply it is the pharmacy when it is not.

5. Claims

Public claims must be truthful, supported, and appropriate to the exact product. Testimonials, blogs, affiliates, and social posts cannot be used to make claims that the company could not lawfully make itself.

6. Compounded drugs

Compounded medications are not FDA-approved. The site must not call them FDA-approved, FDA-licensed, generic versions, or automatically equivalent to an approved medication.

Publication gate

Nothing gets listed just because we can source it.

Before a medication appears publicly, we need the exact human-use legal pathway, clinical role, prescriber model, pharmacy model, state availability, labeling, risks, and advertising language documented.
